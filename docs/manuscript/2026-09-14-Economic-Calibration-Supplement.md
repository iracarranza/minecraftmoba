# Minecraft MOBA Design — Economic Calibration Supplement

**Manuscript supplement • 14 September 2026**

This supplement updates the 10 September manuscript snapshot with later World and Match Systems economic calibration. It is deliberately separate until the full manuscript is regenerated. Canon corrections below supersede incompatible manuscript passages; numerical ore/resource quantities are calibration fixtures unless explicitly labeled established.

## A. Minecraft-first economic doctrine

**Established direction.** Preserve vanilla Minecraft's underlying behavior wherever possible. Add recognition, eligibility, thresholds, spatial rules, renewal, progression interaction, and network effects before replacing intrinsic behavior. Resource relevance should preferably emerge from rational actors responding to match state rather than from explicit declarations that a resource is currently important.

Useful analysis separates five layers: character capacity; vanilla/intrinsic mechanics; emergent organization; recognized MOBA systems; and explicit integration effects. A physical path is not automatically a Route, chests are not automatically a Supply Line, a farm is not automatically a Development Zone, and a useful workshop is not automatically a Construct. Recognized infrastructure should be evaluated against intelligent organized Minecraft play rather than against an intentionally inefficient baseline.

## B. Canon corrections

### B.1 Primary Materials

**Established, 14 September reconciliation.** Primary Materials are **Copper, Iron, Gold, Diamond, Netherite, and Leather**. Copper's previous exclusion is superseded. This affects any mechanic authored against the Primary Material category, including Kitfighter Salvage where its recipe qualification is met.

### B.2 Offhand

**Established, 14 September reconciliation.** There is no functional offhand slot. Weapons, tools, food, light, buckets, shields, utility, class equipment, resources, and cargo all compete for ordinary inventory/active-item handling. Kitfighter's existing Offhander ability is therefore incompatible with this global rule and requires redesign; no replacement is established by this supplement.

This turns the offhand decision into an economic rule as well as a combat/control rule. Early players cannot be fully provisioned Minecraft generalists while retaining unrestricted cargo capacity.

### B.3 Day/night clock

**Established, 14 September reconciliation.** Day lasts 6 minutes and night lasts 6 minutes. Full cycle: 12 minutes. In a 48-minute analytical match the phases are Day 0–6, Night 6–12, Day 12–18, Night 18–24, Day 24–30, Night 30–36, Day 36–42, Night 42–48.

Worksites open at sunset, giving natural macro pulses at **6, 18, 30, and 42 minutes**. Do not globally accelerate all vanilla simulation ticks merely because the phase clock is shorter.

## C. Inventory as an economy

**Working analytical model.** Usable inventory is total slots minus fixed/mission equipment, provisioning, and intentionally reserved cargo. With no offhand, all carried utility participates in this constraint.

**Economic Breadth Capacity** means the number of distinct economically relevant stacks a player can carry without sacrificing mission equipment or expedition provisioning. This is an analytical metric, not a player-facing stat.

The current proposed universal capacity curve remains non-canon. Its important qualitative result survives: opening inventory should strongly constrain generalism, while midgame capacity increasingly allows a player to behave like a complete Minecraft player and late capacity ceases to bind ordinary missions except through bulk/specialized loads.

## D. Equipment capital and material thresholds

Seven full armor sets cost 168 units of a material tier; seven swords cost 14. Therefore **182 material is the gross universal combat-tier saturation threshold**.

This is not an instantaneous purchase breakpoint. Rational teams allocate stock continuously and asymmetrically: critical occupational tools, high-value combat upgrades, high-exposure armor, broader armor, lower-priority tools, then reserve/infrastructure/class uses according to match state.

Approximately 200–219 material is useful only as an upper operational-maturity region if same-tier occupational tools are included. The earlier ~247-Iron mature-utility benchmark is superseded; utility items such as buckets and shields should be modeled individually as inventory-competing mission equipment rather than bundled into a universal mature kit.

Durability should not be globally compressed. Compress it only where vanilla durability would outlive the meaningful match-scale economic cycle for that item's actual use. Current analysis indicates initial armor capital dominates armor replacement, ordinary sword replacement is secondary, and occupational pick consumption is the strongest candidate for deliberate compression.

## E. Copper

Copper ore drops 2–5 raw Copper, average approximately 3.5 before Fortune/Yield. Approximate expected raw Copper per ore used for calibration: F0 3.5; Fortune/Yield I 4.67; II 6.125; III 7.7. Earlier near-1:1 Copper opportunity calculations are superseded.

**Prototype-Test calibration, not generation quota:** ordinary economically relevant Copper field per team ~45–55 physical ore; early Copper Mining Worksite ~8–12 ore.

Copper's Primary Material status also makes Production relevant to its equipment economy. Under current Kitfighter Salvage, seven armor sets create 28 qualifying crafts. If centralized through a Lv8–15 Kitfighter at the five-Salvage threshold, that fixture returns about 10 Copper. Thus 182 remains the universal gross recipe threshold while Production can lower net expenditure.

## F. Iron

**Prototype-Test calibration, not generation quota:** ordinary economically relevant Iron field per team ~130–140 physical ore; Iron Mining Worksite ~40–50 ore, central sensitivity ~45.

Target relationship: ordinary balanced development reaches majority Iron. Universal Iron normally indicates exceptional material opportunity, economic specialization, or sufficiently mature late play. Material is persistent team stock, so 'four kits worth' does not imply four literal complete kits; mixed-tier allocation is expected.

## G. Economic specialization is not interchangeable

The quantitative pass establishes four different functions that should remain distinct:

- **Efficiency:** exploitation tempo, tool persistence, exposure reduction.
- **Yield:** material amplification of a finite opportunity.
- **Production:** acquired-input to useful-output conversion efficiency.
- **Worksite:** additional or concentrated exceptional opportunity.

Efficiency does not multiply physical geology. It can process available geology faster but cannot alone overcome a finite material ceiling. Yield changes how much material the same geology realizes. Production changes conversion after acquisition. Worksites change available opportunity.

## H. Diamond and Netherite

**Prototype-Test calibration:** ordinary late/deep Diamond ~14–18 physical ore; late Diamond Worksite ~8–12. One full Diamond armor+sword investment costs 26 Diamond. Desired result is roughly one major Diamond investment for a balanced team, perhaps two under strong economic specialization/opportunity, never routine universal Diamond.

**Prototype-Test calibration:** ordinary late Ancient Debris ~4–8 blocks; apex/very-late opportunity ~8–12. Fortune does not amplify Ancient Debris. Four Debris are required per Netherite item upgrade before Gold, template, and Smithing-capability constraints. This fixture supports roughly 3–5 individual apex upgrades in a sufficiently long/developed game rather than teamwide Netherite.

## I. Worksites as phase-sensitive inflation

Worksites are concentrated, phase-sensitive economic inflation, not mandatory permission to participate in a resource tier. Base world economy should remain viable; regenerative opportunity relationships preserve recurring strategically important inputs; Worksites create exceptional concentrated opportunities.

With sunsets at 6/18/30/42, useful test vocabulary is: first sunset Copper/Coal/basic processing; second Iron/enchanting transition; third Gold/Lapis/Diamond/advanced capability; fourth Diamond/Ancient Debris/Smithing relationships. These are **phase vocabularies, not guaranteed contents**. Availability and intensity remain separate tuning axes.

Players should be able to win and exploit immediately, delay exploitation for stronger Yield at greater exposure, partially exploit to solve immediate demand, ignore a site, or lose it and continue through the ordinary economy at a disadvantage.

## J. Resource coverage hypothesis

**Prototype-Test.** Current analytical coverage bands are: very high ordinary coverage (~90%+ competitive ordinary demand) for wood/basic food ecology/coal/ordinary construction stone; high ordinary or strategically amplifiable (~75–100%) for Copper/sand/gravel/clay/common livestock/wheat; controlled strategic (~55–80%) for Iron/Redstone/Lapis/specialty crops and livestock; power-resource coverage (~20–50% of resource-specific saturation demand) for Gold/Diamond/high-value enchanting inputs; and very low ordinary coverage where appropriate for apex resources.

These percentages are not hard worldgen targets. They are hypotheses for testing whether ordinary play works while specialization and exceptional geography remain consequential.

## K. Current tier thesis

**Working design target:** universal Copper through ordinary team development; majority Iron through ordinary team development; universal Iron through exceptional opportunity, economic specialization, or sufficiently mature late play; selective Diamond capital; a few Netherite apex item upgrades.

## L. XP calibration frontier

Exact XP yields remain unresolved. Existing XP-band indices remain relative balance targets: Lv1–6 1.000×; Lv7–12 1.350×; Lv13–19 1.875×; Lv20–24 2.575×; Lv25–30 3.250×. A normalized minute-to-level curve used in analysis is a sensitivity fixture, not a schedule that activity rewards should be forced to obey.

The immediately subsequent lost design discussion introduced **Useful Action Units (UAU)** as a basis for bottom-up XP valuation. Its exact definition and equations are not recoverable from current project context. They must not be invented and attributed to the lost discussion. The next progression-calibration pass should reconstruct UAU explicitly from first principles, preserve assumptions, and test whether resulting legitimate XP throughput naturally produces acceptable match progression.

## M. Supersession register

Current-facing material should reject: Copper excluded from Primary Materials; functional/free offhand; old day/night timings; first sunset near 10m or second near 30m; near-1:1 Copper drop assumptions; Efficiency as physical-resource multiplication; ~247 Iron as universal mature-kit demand; routine armor/sword churn as the primary metal sink; Worksites as mandatory tier permission; a normalized minute-to-level sensitivity treated as canon; and any invented UAU formula presented as recovered history.
