# Steps 2 and 4: per-Type yield, and a portfolio a map derives for itself

**24 September 2026.**

## Step 2 — the measurement, at n large enough to act on

From a 59-target batch, after the land-sited Homebase fix:

| Map Type | READY | generated | rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **landmass** | 11 | 25 | **44%** |
| **shattered_coast** | 1 | 31 | **3%** |
| archipelago | 1 | 2 | *n too small* |
| divided_by_a_cut | 0 | 2 | *n too small* |
| divided_by_a_ridge | 0 | 1 | *n too small* |

A **fifteenfold** difference, not the fivefold the earlier n=6/n=10 sample
suggested — which is why the goal required n≥30 before allocating. And
`shattered_coast` consumed **31 of 59 generations**, 53% of the expensive
step, to produce one map.

Budget is now split by measured compile-through rate, with the sample size
carried alongside and a neutral prior for any Type under ten observations.
Every Type keeps a floor of one window: a Type starved to zero can never
revise its own estimate, and 3% is a rate under the *current* Homebase rules.

**[OPEN]** Whether the `shattered_coast` predicate is wrong or the Type
genuinely needs Homebase siting rules other than Default's. n=31 establishes
that the Type as defined is not viable; it does not establish which.

### The Homebase fix that preceded it

`SOCKET_NOT_INDEPENDENTLY_ACCEPTABLE` was 26 of 32 rejections. Testing whether
the prospect-time Homebase measurement predicted it gave a backwards answer —
rejections had a median prospect grade of **0** and playable maps a median of
**3**. Grade 0 was water: callers clamp height to sea level so symmetry reads
the playable surface, which makes every ocean cell exactly flat and therefore
perfectly graded. `homebase_pair` was siting Homebases on open sea.

Overall yield moved 17% → 22%. It helped and did not solve it:
`SOCKET_NOT_INDEPENDENTLY_ACCEPTABLE` remains 48 of 61.

**Third time the sea-level clamp caused a bug** — after `water_structure`
(one land body, zero coastline, every quantile) and `water_fraction` (0.00
across eight seeds). The clamp is right for symmetry and wrong for everything
that must tell land from water.

## Step 4 — the portfolio

A map now derives its regenerative sources from its own geography, and
`renewables` is in `readiness.REQUIRED`. Before this a generated map could
certify READY with no sources at all, and `Renewables` would refuse on it at
runtime — a map the pool called ready on which Development could not begin.
The same shape as the Lair socket that was chosen, measured and never
established.

Validating `derive` against five real compiled maps rather than fixtures found
three bugs, each invisible to fixtures:

**Ranking by the minimum cost across teams** made "near" mean near to
*someone*, so all three near-band slots could land on one team's side. Real
maps produced 12–18 sources where **neither** team could reach one, while a
6-source map passed. The counts were fine and the distribution was not.

**"Near" was a percentile and the opening is not.** A 33rd-percentile near
band sat at costs of 625–1328 while the opening the rest of the compiler uses
ends at 120 — only 3–7 cells of 238 inside it. Two fixtures describing
different regions, so "near" meant one thing in this module and another
everywhere else.

**The floor conflated two layers.** It asked whether a *derived source* sat in
each opening and failed four of five maps, whose openings were full of cows,
pigs and sheep — farther-band kinds the derivation correctly declined to
manifest near. Those openings are not barren. maps.md is explicit that
ordinary vanilla incidence survives beneath strategic manifestations and that
a cow generating in a Hinterland is not a fault. **The ordering governs where a
deliberate manifestation belongs, not whether a team can begin Development.**

The floor is: each team reaches one developable opportunity in its own
opening, **wild or manifested**. Not a count — one team's twelve cannot cover
another's none — and not a species list, which would reimpose the ecological
symmetry doctrine denies. All five maps certify on their own ecology with
**different species per team**, which is the intended case.

### And one the fixtures could not have caught

`_renewables` merged `{**derived, **certified}`. Both carry a `sources` key:
`derive` returns the list of Source specs, `certify` returns their count. The
spread let the count win, so the binding handed the runtime the integer 13
where it needed thirteen sources — and `readiness` only inspects `certified`,
so every map would have passed. Caught by exercising the real binding path
against real maps before the batch that depended on it ran.

## A measurement note

A batch reported 3h52m elapsed against 1h24m of internal wall time. The
machine had been closed and the process suspended. `foundry_run` measures its
own wall clock, so the 9.29 READY/hour figure was unaffected — had elapsed
time been quoted it would have read 3.3/hour and invented a threefold
slowdown that never happened.

**Second occurrence this session.** The first was diagnosed as swap thrashing
before the same explanation turned out to apply. On this machine, suspended
time is the hypothesis to reach for before performance ones.
