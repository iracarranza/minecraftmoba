# First near-miss rescue test — seed 930010639 (`frozen/open`)

**22 September doctrine correction — interpretation superseded, measurements
retained:** 930010639's **0.3173** is permitted N/S Wilderness difference under
one terrain metric, not a proven competitive deficit. Unchanged terrain after
opportunity placement is not a failed rescue: authoring is not required to erase
it. `balance_asymmetry` is estimated access to selected opportunities, not proof
of fairness. 991 versus 2,285 measures qualification under this prototype search,
not general authorability or economic harm. Neither optimizer compensation nor
terrain-gap rejection is justified without the relevant contract/gameplay
evidence. The question is: **Does this permitted wilderness asymmetry cause a
competitive consequence that the authored-opportunity model fails to capture?**
The historical text below records the earlier interpretation; the
[current audit](../audit/2026-09-22-spatial-doctrine.md) supersedes its deficit,
rescue and next-step claims.

Axes, held fixed throughout: **W–E is the regional axis**, where `frozen/open`
contrast is intentional. **N–S is the team axis**, where a deficit decides
whether a seed is usable. Nothing below gives one team a different biome.

## Does placement repair the identified deficit? No.

This is the test that matters, and the answer is negative.

Each placement records which team it favours. If authoring were repairing a
south-side deficit, the deficient seed should place more opportunities toward
the south than a balanced seed does. It does not:

| | south-biased share of placements |
|---|---|
| Alpha (balanced) | 0.36 – 0.41 |
| 930010639 (south deficient) | 0.38 – 0.42 |

Essentially identical. The optimizer does not steer toward the deficient team.

**The structural reason is that the two quantities are not the same measurement.**
`balance_asymmetry` is computed from travel-cost differences between the teams
*to the opportunities it places*. The diagnosed deficit is the south having less
near-depth workable land, which is a property of the terrain's depth bands.
Authoring places opportunities; it does not create depth bands. So the
post-authoring accessible-land gap is **still 0.3173** — unchanged by
construction, not merely unimproved.

The residual deficit is therefore the entire original deficit. What authoring
achieved is equal *access to what it placed*, on top of terrain that remains
unequal.

## What this establishes

**A near-miss can pass the balance filter without the thing that made it a
near-miss being repaired.** That is a real failure mode of the current pipeline,
and it was only visible because the deficit was diagnosed independently before
authoring rather than inferred from the final scalar.

It does not mean the rescue is worthless. Equal access to placed opportunities
is a genuine property, and 0.0161 is better than the map in play. It means the
scalar is not evidence about the deficit, and should not be read as such.

## Next step

Two options, and they are different claims.

**Make the optimizer aware of the deficit.** `balance_asymmetry` could weight
placements by the receiving team's accessible-land shortfall, so compensating
placement is what the objective rewards. This is a change to an existing
objective rather than a new system, but it is a design decision about what
balance means and should be taken deliberately.

**Or accept that terrain deficits are not authoring targets**, and treat
accessible-land gap as a screening gate alongside the physical arm. That would
make 930010639 a rejection despite scoring 0.0161.

The measurement needed to choose between them is the same either way: whether a
0.3173 accessible-land gap is felt in play. Nothing in the analysis can answer
that.

## Not pursued

The physical-failure arm, as instructed. No seed in the sample exhibits it.
