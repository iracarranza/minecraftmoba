expected usable food value generated by the renewable opportunity per regeneration cycle.

That lets the patch/herd size vary according to the actual Minecraft resource, while rarity remains a separate worldgen variable.

Yes. Wheat is not actually the superior vanilla crop for raw food production. Its popularity is partly habit/legacy, partly accessibility, and partly that wheat participates in more adjacent vanilla systems.
For our purposes, the distinction is useful because carrots are better agriculture, while bread is better cargo.
Why wheat is so common in ordinary Minecraft
A few things favor wheat that are not “wheat makes more food”:
- Starter access is trivial. Wheat seeds come from breaking grass, so virtually any overworld start can begin wheat farming immediately. Carrots normally require finding an existing source such as a village or certain mob drops.
- Wheat has broad secondary use. It breeds cows and sheep, can make hay bales, and participates in several recipes. Seeds also feed/breed chickens.
- Bread is a good carried food. A bread item restores substantially more hunger/saturation than a carrot, while both stack to 64.
- Wheat was also Minecraft's original/default crop for a long time, so there is a substantial practice/tutorial tradition behind “make a wheat farm.”
But if you already possess carrots and are measuring food produced per planted farmland block, carrots are dramatically better.
Production versus carrying
Using the values from the previous pass:
Bread:
5 hunger + 6 saturation ≈ 11 effective nourishment

Carrot:
3 hunger + 3.6 saturation ≈ 6.6
So per inventory slot:
64 bread   ≈ 704 nourishment
64 carrots ≈ 422.4 nourishment
Bread carries about:
704 / 422.4 ≈ 1.67×
