# Capability audit — where design outruns the engine

Date: 2026-09-12
Scope: full manuscript read at `b9ffc25`. Datapack-only target, Java 1.21.9+.

This document ranks systems by **technical danger**: how likely the mechanism
cannot be built in a vanilla datapack, multiplied by how much design rests on
it, multiplied by how late the failure would be noticed.

It does not change any design. It records where a design assumes a capability
that has not been demonstrated.

## Tag introduced

**[TECHNICAL RISK]** — this passage assumes an engine capability that is
unproven or known to be unavailable. Distinct from [OPEN], which marks an
undecided design question. A passage can be fully decided as design and still
carry technical risk.

---

## 0. The shared dependency nobody owns: block provenance

Vanilla does not record whether a block was generated by the world or placed by
a player. Cobblestone is cobblestone.

**Five systems assume it independently:**

| System | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Anti-farming XP (§5.2) | mining a placed-then-remined block must not pay |
| Mole's Sinkhole (§9.3) | natural terrain only; player construction survives |
| Construct designation (§6.18) | "these **authored** blocks constitute one Construct" |
| Structural Integrity (§6.2.4) | applies to *incorporated* Construction Blocks |
| Development Zones (§3.3, §6.19) | distinguish natural patches, ordinary farms, recognized Development |

It is solvable — detect placement, record positions — but that means persistent
per-block state over the whole map with no natural eviction policy. Chapter 13
records no such contract; each chapter assumes it locally as though free.

**This is the first thing to prove or kill.** Five systems fail together.

---

## Tier 1 — most dangerous

### 1. Structural Integrity (§6.2.4)

Now the canonical intrinsic Construct benefit, so the entire Construction
payoff rests on it. **Vanilla has no hook.** Block hardness and blast
resistance are not per-instance data-driven, and a datapack cannot cancel a
block break. The only implementation is detect-and-restore: the block breaks,
the attacker gets the drop and the time, then it returns. Explosions are worse.

*Escape hatch:* express integrity as **rebuild, not resistance** — reinforced
blocks self-repair after a delay unless the attacker keeps working. Honest,
legible, implementable.

### 2. Structural defeat validation (§7.3)

Percentage-of-blocks is correctly rejected, but the replacement requires
structural or topological analysis of an arbitrarily damaged, player-modified
building. Flood-fill is explicitly discouraged in §6.18 and is expensive.
Structures will be tunnelled under and walled over by existing class kits.

This sits on the **victory path** and is currently marked only [OPEN].

*Escape hatch:* the map is authored, so define **functional components at known
coordinates** — supports, cores, machinery — and defeat as component
destruction. Cheap, legible, and makes "meaningful" structural rather than
volumetric.

### 3. Anti-farming and XP attribution (§5.2)

XP is the progression spine and no duplicate-credit rule exists. Statistics
cannot distinguish mined-natural from mined-replaced, or world pickup from a
teammate's handover. Depends on provenance (§0).

### 4. Effective Hunger above 20 (§5.5)

§13.6 already concedes vanilla exposes no locomotion-only exhaustion scaling
and that any datapack version is an approximation. §5.5 nonetheless specifies
multipliers to four decimals on a mechanism that cannot hit them. §13.5.1
records that current Hunger enforcement already produces visible artifacts.

### 5. Inventory capacity (§13.5.2)

Per-tick eviction, with locked slots still visible. There is no native slot
locking. This is a **universal** feature affecting every player from Level 1,
so friction here costs far more than friction in one class.

*Escape hatch:* occupy locked slots with a placeholder item rather than
evicting from them. Pre-emptive instead of reactive; vanilla pickup and
shift-click route around full slots automatically.

## Tier 2 — hard, escape hatches exist

**6. Unloaded-chunk operation.** Supply Lines pulsing between distant Copper
Chests, Development Zones recalculating, Worksites and Mob Swarms acting away
from players. Forceloading is limited and costly. Not recorded in Chapter 13,
and it silently caps how far infrastructure can project.

**7. Mining Outpost generate-then-reveal (§7.5.2).** Placing new ore in
"eligible surrounding geology" needs a volume scan plus a rule that never
overwrites player construction — provenance again.

**8. Region-scale measurement.** Constructs, Development Zones and structural
defeat all need volume counting. Individually fine; collectively they need a
shared scan budget that does not exist.

**9. Event cancellation generally.** Structural Integrity, Sinkhole's no-build
area, armour locking, infrastructure protection all want to *prevent*. Vanilla
only detects and reverts. Adventure mode with `can_place_on`/`can_break` is the
only true prevention and is too restrictive for a building game.

---

## Two failure patterns

**Numerical design ahead of mechanism.** Exhaustion multipliers, integration
multipliers to three decimals, Capacity values, XP bands — all specified
precisely on unbuilt or approximate mechanisms. Chapter 13 knows this in the
abstract; the numbers elsewhere carry no inline caveat.

**Capabilities assumed independently by multiple chapters.** Provenance, chunk
loading and event cancellation each appear as a local assumption in three to
five places and never as a shared contract. This is exactly the shape of the
`input` predicate failure: a reasonable-looking assumption, untested, and
load-bearing in more places than anyone tracked.

---

## Recommended order

1. Prove or kill **block provenance**.
2. Re-express **Structural Integrity** as rebuild-not-resist, or accept
   detect-and-restore and say so in the design.
3. Replace **structural defeat** with functional components.
4. Establish a **shared technical contracts** section (now §13.8) so these stop
   being re-assumed per chapter.
